# The Fogg Behaviour Model for Exercise Adherence

## TIER 5.2: B = MAP

The sources apply the **B = MAP** (Behaviour = Motivation × Ability × Prompt) formula to ensure patients actually do their rehab.

For a behaviour to occur, **all three** must be present at the same moment:

- **B**ehaviour
- **M**otivation
- **A**bility
- **P**rompt

If any one is missing, the behaviour does not happen.

## Motivation

This is kept high by:

- Linking exercises directly to **internal motivators** (e.g., gardening, picking up grandkids)
- Ensuring the patient hits **milestones quickly** to experience a "fast start"

A patient who can connect today's exercise to their dream outcome is far more motivated than one who is just told "do these for your back."

## Ability

Exercises must be:

- **Pain-free** — pain reduces ability instantly
- **Designed so the patient can "feel the difference" immediately** after performing them
- **Easy to fit into daily life** rather than requiring dedicated gym time

The lower the friction, the higher the ability — and the more likely the behaviour occurs.

## Prompt / Trigger

You must identify a **specific daily event** to act as a reminder for the exercise. The prompt converts intention into action.

## Real Examples

### Example 1: Soleus & Hamstring Engagement

- **Exercise:** "Squash an orange through the midfoot"
- **Prompt:** Every time the patient walks up the stairs
- **Why:** Engages the soleus and hamstrings using a daily, unavoidable trigger.

### Example 2: Respiratory Down-Regulation

- **Exercise:** Humming (to increase exhalation time)
- **Prompt:** Triggered by a "useless" or stressful thought
- **Why:** Helps the patient return to a "rest and digest" state during moments their nervous system would otherwise spiral.

### Example 3: Reflexive Shoulder Stability

- **Exercise:** Wrist taps against a wall while leaning forward
- **Prompt:** Every time the patient walks past a wall in their home
- **Why:** Creates reflexive (bottom-up) perturbations for shoulder stability without requiring dedicated time.

## Why This Matters for the RPM

Every exercise prescribed in the Reactive Graph should pass the B = MAP test:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Motivation | Is this exercise tied to a milestone the patient cares about? |
| Ability | Can the patient do it pain-free, today, in their environment? |
| Prompt | Is there a specific daily trigger attached to it? |

If any answer is "no," the exercise will not be done, regardless of how clinically perfect it is.
