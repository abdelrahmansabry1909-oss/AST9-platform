# The Complex Case Study (Chapter 17)

## Question 6.1 — The Complete Case Walkthrough

### Patient Presentation

A **75-year-old female** with persistent back pain for over **eight years**, which had been progressively worsening. She had a history of a bad **right-ankle sprain approximately ten years prior**.

**Previous failed treatments included:**
- Physiotherapy (massage, core exercises, pain education)
- Chiropractic manipulation
- Massage therapy
- Pilates

### Subjective Assessment Findings

- **Dream Outcome:** Gardening for two hours per day
- **Internal Motivators:** The need to lift / help her husband off his chair daily and carry shopping bags up a flight of stairs
- **Stressors:** Chronic back pain that prevented her from sitting to watch TV or sleeping through the night

### Objective Assessment Findings

#### Movement Strategies

- Poor ribcage mobility into flexion with pain at the start of movement
- Poor right-leg intent during sit-to-stands, using the low back excessively to compensate

#### Three-Layer Assessment Findings

| Layer | Finding |
|---|---|
| Generic | Ribcage and leg-intent issues |
| Passive | No upper-limb issues, but highlighted the previous ankle injury's impact |
| Coordinative | Poor force steadiness in the right leg, right-side frontal plane (glutes), and the abdominals |

### Treatment Plan Design

The **root problem** was defined as the patient not using her right leg enough, causing the low back to overwork.

Using the **80/20 rule**:
- **80%** of focus on increasing right-leg load tolerance
- **20%** on desensitising back symptoms

### Milestones Hit at Each Session

| Sessions | Module | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions 1–2 | Module 2 | Restoring right-leg load tolerance through top-down cues — immediate help with husband transfers and stairs |
| Next Sessions | Module 5 | Improving ribcage mobility and breathing to help her sit and sleep better once the back was less "overworked" by the legs |
| Final Sessions | Modules 6 & 7 | Bridging the gap from low-level to high-level rehab — building "buffer capacity" and resilience for the two-hour gardening goal |

### Sequential Application of the 8 Pillars

| Pillar | Action |
|---|---|
| Pillar 1 | Established rapport and surfaced internal motivators (husband, shopping, gardening) |
| Pillar 2 | Cross-linked findings to the patient's story (old ankle → leg under-use → back overwork) |
| Pillar 3 | Effective Explanation — the leg/back load imbalance explained simply |
| Pillar 4 | Treatment Plan Design — milestones mapped on a graded exposure ladder |
| Pillar 5 | Hands-on treatment for "fast start" symptom relief |
| Pillar 6 | Low-Level Rehab to engage the right leg with top-down cues |
| Pillar 7 | Bridging the Gap to reflexive, bottom-up movement |
| Pillar 8 | Strength & Conditioning to build buffer capacity for gardening |

### Outcome

The patient moved from **guarded, top-down movement** to **"thoughtless, fearless movement."**

She achieved her goal of **gardening for two-plus hours** and could tolerate daily life without flare-ups because the **root cause** (leg load tolerance) was resolved — not the symptom (back pain).

### Key Lessons from This Case

1. **The painful site is rarely the root cause.** Back pain came from leg under-use, which came from an ankle sprain ten years prior.
2. **Internal motivators drive adherence.** "Gardening" + "husband" + "shopping" gave the patient reasons to push through the work.
3. **The 80/20 rule resists the urge to symptom-chase.** 80% on the leg, 20% on the back — even though the patient came in saying "my back hurts."
4. **Sequential pillar application mattered.** Skipping any pillar (especially Pillar 3 — Effective Explanation) would have lost the patient at session 3 or 4 when the pain eased.
5. **The graded exposure ladder gave a destination.** Without milestones tied to gardening, the patient would have stopped at "pain gone" — which is not the same as "resilient."
