# The Graded Exposure Ladder

The Graded Exposure Ladder is the core system used to transition a patient from Point A (their current state of pain and limitation) to Point B (their "dream outcome" or destination). By using this framework, you move from solving symptoms to building a resilient person through a logical, data-driven progression.

## 1.1 The Ladder Structure

The ladder is a visual representation of load tolerance and movement quality, moving from bed-based interventions to high-load, real-world activities.

### ASCII Representation of the Ladder

```
[ DESTINATION: POINT B ] -> Thoughtless, Fearless Movement & Resilience [4, 10]
          |
[ STAGE 5: HIGH-LOAD ] -> Return to Running/Sport (Max Intent) [11, 12]
          |
[ STAGE 4: BOTTOM-UP ] -> Reflexive movement, chaotic environments [13-15]
          |
[ STAGE 3: BRIDGING  ] -> Mixing top-down cues with dynamic stability [13, 16]
          |
[ STAGE 2: STANDING  ] -> Loading in gravity; challenging base of support [17, 18]
          |
[ STAGE 1: ENTRY     ] -> Bed-based; desensitising and submaximal tests [17, 19]
          |
[ POINT A: START     ] -> Current symptoms and motor adaptations [1, 3, 20]
```

### Stages & Representation

- **Bottom Levels:** Focus on desensitising symptoms and restoring submaximal load tolerance (e.g., on the treatment bed).
- **Middle Levels:** Introduce gravity and standing progressions where the nervous system must "self-organise" to maintain balance.
- **Top Levels:** Build resilience and confidence through high-speed, high-load activities like hopping and running.

### Transition Criteria

A patient moves up only after they have "earned the right" by passing specific KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) or tripwire screens without negative reactions like pain or reduced motor output.

### Definitions of Cues

- **Top-down Cues:** Conscious, internal or external cues (e.g., "squash an orange into the floor") that require active thinking to override motor adaptations.
- **Bottom-up Cues:** Reflexive movements where the brain controls the "intent" but the spinal cord and peripheral tissues handle the reaction to the environment without conscious planning.
- **Thoughtless, Fearless Movement:** The ultimate goal where a person moves without any second thought or worry about their problem returning.

## 1.2 Tripwires & Decision Gates

Tripwires are built-in safety nets that remove emotion from the clinical decision-making process.

### Midfoot Bridge Hold (Bed-to-Standing Tripwire)

The patient bridges up one inch off the bed for 30 seconds while pushing only through the midfoot.

- **Check:** It stresses the soleus and hamstrings. Cramping or inability to hold indicates the nervous system still perceives a load tolerance threat.
- **Logic:** If pass → progress to standing; if fail → remain at bed-level and address submaximal load tolerance.

### Hopping Progressions (Standing-to-Running Tripwire)

Includes leaps, hop-and-stick, and continuous hops.

- **Check:** Hopping generates greater ground reaction forces than jogging. If a patient cannot hop with "thoughtless, fearless movement," they are not ready to run.
- **Logic:** If pass → start the running graded exposure; if fail (pain/poor landing) → more work needed in the bridging gap phase.

### Removing Emotion

Using these screens prevents the therapist from "guessing" or caving to pressure from a coach or patient who "feels fine" on the bed but lacks the capacity for real-world load.

## 1.3 The 80/20 Rule & Inversion Principle

The sources define an 80/20 rule (rather than 60/40) for treatment strategy.

### 80/20 Time Allocation

- **20% of time:** Dedicated to desensitising symptoms (the second-order consequences).
- **80% of time:** Focused on the true driver or root cause (what is not doing its job based on the patient's story).

### Inversion Principle

This involves looking at the problem in reverse — starting at the destination and working backward to identify the necessary milestones.

- **The Inversion Question:** Ask the patient, "What needs to happen before you are confident to do [dream outcome]?"
- **Milestones:** The answers to the inversion question (e.g., "be able to walk up stairs," "be able to hop") become the milestone labels on the ladder.
- **The Fast Start:** This concept focuses on hitting the first milestone early to provide a "big emotional win". Designing the first session to show immediate progress (e.g., via top-down cues that reduce pain during a task) secures the buy-in needed for the patient to stay the course.
