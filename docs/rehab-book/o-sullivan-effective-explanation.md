# The Effective Explanation Framework

## TIER 5.1: The 3-Step Effective Explanation

This framework takes the confusion out of the clinical situation by communicating three things:

1. **The Problem**
2. **The Solution**
3. **The Plan**

## Point A vs Point B

You must demonstrate **Point B** (the destination), which is what *should* be happening (e.g., all muscles sharing the load equally), versus **Point A** (the current reality), which shows the motor adaptations causing the issue.

## Visual Diagram Method

The sources recommend using simple stick-figure diagrams or percentage representations to visually prove the root cause.

**Example:**

- A "healthy" leg where four muscles do 25% work each
- vs. the patient's leg where one muscle does 70% and others do 10%

This visual contrast makes the imbalance immediately obvious to the patient.

## Realistic Timeframes

By mapping out milestones, you provide a logical justification for the number of sessions required (e.g., 6–8), ensuring the patient understands that pain relief is only the beginning of the process.

## Pre-framing Technique

This involves addressing objections **before** they arise.

**Example:** Pre-frame the fact that a patient will want to run in session 4 because they are pain-free, but explain they must hit a "hopping milestone" first to avoid a flare-up.

## The 3-Step Structure in Practice

| Step | What to Communicate | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem | Why pain is happening | Visual stick-figure showing imbalanced load distribution |
| 2. Solution | What needs to change | Show Point B vs Point A — what muscles need to start sharing the load |
| 3. Plan | How we'll get there | Mapped milestones on the graded exposure ladder + realistic session count |

## Why This Matters

A patient who **understands** their problem and the path to recovery has the buy-in needed to stay engaged through the inevitable ups and downs of rehab. The effective explanation is the bridge from S.O.S. (Sense of Symptoms) to B.A.R. (Buy-in, Adherence, Retention).
