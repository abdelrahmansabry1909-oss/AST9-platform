# The Clinical Framework for Patient Assessment and Retention

The following frameworks are extracted from the source material, primarily Chapter 5, and detail the clinical and psychological systems used to identify root causes and ensure patient progression.

## 2.1 The Subjective Assessment Framework

The aim of the subjective assessment is thirteenfold, designed to move beyond symptoms to the "person's story".

### The 13 Aims of the Subjective Assessment

1. **Establish rapport and authority:** Use eye contact, tonality, and empathy to put the patient at ease while maintaining an "expert authority".
2. **Make the patient feel safe, understood, accepted, and in control:** Set expectations for the session so they know what to expect.
3. **Identify the most important thing the patient wants:** Use the formula: Happiness = Reality – Expectations.
4. **Establish internal problems:** Determine what the external problem (pain) is preventing them from doing.
5. **Establish the mechanism of injury:** Identify peripheral tissues involved and potential protective responses.
6. **Establish aggravating and easing factors:** Use these to piece together which tissues are shortening or lengthening.
7. **Get a clear timeline of stressors:** Map previous physical injuries and emotional traumas in chronological order.
8. **Identify potential objections or tripwires:** Find "hidden objections" that could sabotage the outcome.
9. **Identify perceived likelihood of achievement:** Understand the patient's starting level of motivation and belief.
10. **Identify opportunities to "start fast":** Find ways to add value immediately with minimal effort or sacrifice.
11. **Rule out red flags:** Screen for sinister pathologies like bladder/bowel dysfunction or constant night pain.
12. **Identify yellow flags and medical history:** Use medications (e.g., for anxiety or asthma) as clues to a sensitised nervous system.
13. **Recap the patient's story:** Repeat the story back to ensure they feel heard and to help you "connect the dots".

### Specific Probing Questions

#### Internal Motivators
- "What is the pain stopping you from doing?"
- "How is the pain affecting your daily life?"
- "What was the defining moment for you when you decided to take action and get this sorted?"

#### Dream Outcome
- "What's the most important thing to you that you want out of today's session?"
- "What is your definition of success?"

#### Mechanism & Timeline
Establish the injury timeline chronologically, specifically asking about "major times of trauma," "prolonged stress," and "typical daily routines" to identify sensitised respiratory or nervous systems.

#### Objections / Tripwires
- "What have you tried previously?"
- "What helped? What didn't help?"
- "Is there anything that makes it worse?"

#### Perceived Likelihood
- "On a scale of 1 to 10, how confident are you that this problem will get sorted once and for all?"
- "On a scale of 1 to 10... how important is this for you to get sorted right now?"

## 2.2 The Value Equation & Patient Psychology

The source material emphasises that a "sale" (or patient commitment) only happens when value exceeds price.

### D.O.M.S. Concept

This stands for **Dream Outcome, Milestones, Start Fast**. The patient buys the long-term "Dream Outcome," but they stay because of the short-term experience of hitting "Milestones" early (the "Start Fast").

### 5-Step Patient-Retention Method

1. **Know your result/benefit:** Focus on the internal motivator (solving the life problem) rather than just the external pain.
2. **Map out milestones:** Break the journey into achievable markers to increase the perceived likelihood of success.
3. **Pre-frame:** Address common objections (like the desire to run too early) before they arise to maintain authority.
4. **Have a clear next step:** Never leave a session without the patient knowing exactly what the next milestone is.
5. **Get great results:** This is the foundation for all reviews and referrals.

### The Patient Journey Phases

Patients move through four phases:

1. **Starting** (starts treatment) →
2. **Nervous / Skeptical** (experiencing self-doubt) →
3. **Solidifying** (noticing positive results / small victories) →
4. **Expanding & Improving** (desire for bigger victories)

### Prevention of Drop-offs

Patients typically drop off at sessions 3-4 because the pain (external motivator) eases, and they lack the internal motivation to continue. To prevent this, you must:
- Set expectations in session one
- Use inversion to work backward from their "Point B"
- Ensure they are "selling you" on the next milestone rather than you selling them a session

## 2.3 The S.O.S. B.A.R. D.O.M.S. Triangle

This triangle represents the three critical sides needed to create "raving fans".

### S.O.S. (Sense of Symptoms)

The ability to use assessment to find the true cause, which provides the confidence to explain the problem and solution to the patient.

### B.A.R. (Buy-in, Adherence, Retention)

Achieving this allows the therapist the necessary time to bridge the gap from low-level to high-level rehab and reach the "thoughtless, fearless movement" stage.

### D.O.M.S. (Dream Outcome, Milestones, Start Fast)

This delivers the ultimate result by prescribing the right exercises at the right time to hit milestones and create raving fans.

### Interconnection

Making sense of symptoms (S.O.S.) builds the confidence needed to get buy-in (B.A.R.), which provides the time required to deliver the milestones and final result (D.O.M.S.).

### 3 Simple Ways to Increase Value Instantly

1. **Solve an actual problem worth solving:** Don't just treat a "weak glute"; solve the patient's inability to pick up their grandkids.
2. **Deliver results quicker:** Use clinical reasoning to identify the "first milestone" the patient can feel in everyday life.
3. **Reduce your patient's effort:** Fit rehab into their daily life using "triggers" (e.g., doing an exercise every time they use the stairs) rather than relying on motivation alone.
